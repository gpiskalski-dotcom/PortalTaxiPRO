Poland has over 58,000 taxi licences and more than 340,000 licence extracts (one per licensed vehicle), and its app-based market is organised in a way that differs from many other European countries. This guide explains its structure, rules and main players. It is based on public registers, company disclosures and media reports; where a figure is an estimate, we say so.

## How the system works: licence, vehicle, driver

Passenger transport by taxi is regulated by the Road Transport Act (*ustawa o transporcie drogowym*) and by local licensing procedures.

- **Taxi licence.** Issued by a municipality (*gmina*) to a business, for its area, for 2 to 50 years.
- **Licence extract** (*wypis z licencji*). A document assigned to a specific vehicle. Poland had about 5.9 times more extracts than licences at the end of 2024, which shows how much of the market is organised in multi-vehicle fleets.
- **Area rule.** A driver may take a passenger outside the licence area but may not pick up new passengers there, except on a return trip or a pre-booked order. Neighbouring municipalities can already agree on a shared licence area; the industry debate is about making such metropolitan licences more common.
- **Driver requirements.** Since 17 June 2024 drivers working through apps must hold a **Polish** driving licence. Intermediaries must verify each driver in person before the first ride, check them in the Central Register of Drivers and keep copies of documents.

There is no separate "ride-hailing licence" as in some countries. App rides in Poland are carried out under taxi licences, often through a fleet partner.

## Apps took over demand

Apps have become the main channel for ordering a taxi. Freenow said in 2025 that apps generated over 70% of taxi rides. The three large platforms are **Bolt**, **Uber** and **Freenow by Lyft**. The Polish operator **iTaxi** focuses on business clients.

- **Uber** has operated in Poland since 2014.
- **Bolt** recommends fleet partners in dozens of cities. In July 2026 it launched **Bolt Drive** in Warsaw – app-based car rental with almost 400 hybrid Toyotas – and announced over PLN 150m (~EUR 34m) of investment in Poland by the end of 2027. Bolt Drive is a rental service for customers, not cars for ride-hailing.
- **Freenow** was bought by the US company **Lyft** for about EUR 175m; the deal closed on 31 July 2025. Lyft plans to start replacing the Freenow brand with Lyft in Poland in Q1 2027. Freenow says it works with over 10,000 active drivers in Poland.
- **iTaxi** has grown by acquiring regional taxi companies (16 acquisitions, including Ele Taxi in November 2025). Its revenue in 2025 was PLN 90.7m (~EUR 20.7m).

Platform commissions were typically described as about 20–25% of the fare (Jagiellonian Institute, 2023). None of the companies publish their share of the Polish market.

## The Polish specialty: fleet and settlement partners

The feature that most surprises foreign observers is the **fleet partner** (*partner flotowy*). Many app drivers do not contract with the platform directly. They work through a company that stands between the driver and the app and provides some or all of the following:

- the taxi licence and vehicle extract,
- settlement of earnings and taxes,
- a car for weekly or shift rental,
- paperwork, medical checks and, for foreigners, help with residence and work permits.

There are several types: pure settlement partners (the driver has their own car), fleet partners with their own cars, specialised car-rental firms and hybrid groups. Bolt sends drivers without a business or licence to its recommended partners. Freenow requires its partners to have a business, a taxi licence in the city and more than one driver.

The best-documented large group is **Eternis** (including Beter), with about 1,500 cars in Poland according to business media and a joint project with Bolt for a dedicated fleet of at least 1,000 vehicles. Other large multi-city partners include DEPartner, ERES Partner, EG Group and NMS Korpo; their published driver and car numbers are self-declared and not audited.

For the regulatory consequences of this model, see the Platform Work Directive section below.

## The 2024 shock: the Polish driving licence requirement

The series of amendments known as **"lex Uber"** gradually moved compliance duties onto platforms and their partners. The most disruptive step came on **17 June 2024**: drivers working through apps must now hold a Polish driving licence.

- Before the change, foreigners made up over two thirds of app drivers, mostly from Ukraine (Jagiellonian Institute).
- After the change, the media reported falls in driver supply of about 30% at Uber, about 15% at Freenow and about 26% at Bolt in Warsaw. Fares rose by about 20–25% (Interia, Money.pl).
- In 2025 Freenow said about one third of its drivers were foreigners.
- Penalties for breaching the verification duties can reach PLN 1m (~EUR 228,000) and apply both to intermediaries and to people performing the transport (Forsal.pl).

Further tightening is planned. Bill **UD431** of the Ministry of Infrastructure, added to the government's work programme on 7 July 2026, includes a criminal-record certificate from the country of origin for drivers from outside the EU and EFTA, a nationwide driver ID, a duty for intermediaries to check the vehicle's roadworthiness test and taxi liability insurance, and lower, proportionate penalties.

Licensing speed differs strongly between cities. According to Freenow's Polish managing director, a taxi licence in Warsaw takes about four months and a driver ID over a year, while in Kraków and the Tricity it takes two to three working days (*Rzeczpospolita*, 3.11.2026).

## Consolidation

Small, one-person taxi businesses are disappearing while intermediaries and large fleets grow.

- In H1 2025 over 1,600 taxi businesses were removed from the register and only 628 were added (Dun & Bradstreet for *Rzeczpospolita*).
- Over ten years the number of taxi businesses fell by about 10%, and by about 30% since 2009 (same source).
- Major deals: Lyft–Freenow (2025) and iTaxi–Ele Taxi (November 2025).

## What comes next: the EU Platform Work Directive

Directive (EU) 2024/2831 must be transposed by **2 December 2026**. Poland will miss the deadline: the government bill (UC160) had not been adopted by the government as of 4 November 2026.

The bill provides for:

- a **presumption of employment** – the platform must prove that a driver is not an employee;
- **joint and several liability** of the platform and the intermediary that pays the driver – in Poland usually the fleet partner;
- human oversight of automated decisions such as account blocks.

For the Polish model, this is a test of the whole structure: if drivers are reclassified as employees, social security contributions, paid leave and sick pay come into play. Employers' organisations criticise the bill; its final shape is not yet known.

## Key terms

- **Taxi licence** (*licencja taxi*) – permission for a business to carry passengers by taxi in a given municipality, for 2 to 50 years.
- **Licence extract** (*wypis z licencji*) – a document assigned to one vehicle operating under a business's licence. The number of extracts is the best proxy for the formal size of the fleet.
- **Licence area** (*obszar licencji*) – the municipality (or group of municipalities) where a taxi may pick up passengers.
- **Fleet partner** (*partner flotowy*) – a company between the driver and the app that provides the licence, vehicle and often the car.
- **Settlement partner** (*partner rozliczeniowy*) – a partner that handles a driver's earnings and paperwork when the driver has their own car.
- **Intermediary** (*pośrednik*) – under Polish law, a company that arranges passenger transport, such as a platform or a fleet partner. Intermediaries carry verification duties.
- **Lex Uber** – informal name for the amendments to the Road Transport Act that regulate app-based passenger transport.
- **Polish driving licence requirement** – since 17 June 2024, app drivers must hold a Polish driving licence.
- **Central Register of Drivers** (*Centralna Ewidencja Kierowców*) – the state register used to check that a driving licence is valid.
- **Driver ID** (*identyfikator kierowcy*) – a badge identifying a taxi driver; bill UD431 proposes a nationwide driver ID.
- **Presumption of employment** (*domniemanie stosunku pracy*) – the rule from the Platform Work Directive that a platform worker is presumed to be an employee unless proven otherwise.
- **Road Transport Inspection (ITD)** – the state authority that, together with the police, checks taxis and app drivers on the road.
