Lyft, the US company that has owned Freenow since 2025, will start replacing the Freenow brand with its own in Poland in Q1 2027. In this text we focus on what the change means for the Polish market; Lyft's global figures are given only for scale.

## Lyft in brief

Lyft is a US ride-hailing platform based in San Francisco and listed on Nasdaq. Until 2025 it operated only in North America (USA and Canada), where it is the number two after Uber. Its CEO is David Risher.

In Q2 2026 Lyft completed 262.4m rides (+12% y/y), had 30.5m active riders (+17%) and USD 5.5bn of gross bookings (+23%). Revenue was USD 1.84bn and net income USD 50.3m. The company says ride growth came partly from Freenow by Lyft in Europe but does not publish separate figures for that segment. Unlike Uber and Bolt, Lyft does not run food or grocery delivery.

## The Freenow acquisition

Lyft announced the purchase of Freenow on 16 April 2025 for about EUR 175m (about USD 197m) in cash. The sellers were BMW and Mercedes-Benz Mobility, co-owners since the mytaxi days. The deal closed on 31 July 2025.

Freenow operates in over 200 cities in nine countries (Lyft data for 2026): Ireland, the UK, Germany, Greece, Spain, Italy, Poland, France and Austria. After the deal the Freenow team stayed in place and countries are still run by local managers. Lyft also said that in Europe it would stay with taxis – the model Freenow has always been built on.

Lyft is moving all nine Freenow countries to its own app. In June 2025, before the deal closed, Lyft's management had told *Handelsblatt* that the Freenow brand would stay; the single-app plan is a change of course. Lyft says it plans to make the common app widely available in 2027.

## How the change will look in Poland

The rebranding is spread over time and has no end date. Details for Poland were given by Krzysztof Urban, managing director of Freenow by Lyft in Poland, in an interview with *Rzeczpospolita* (3 November 2026):

- **Since the acquisition (2025):** the app runs as "Freenow by Lyft". Accounts, ride history and payment methods work as before.
- **From November 2026:** roaming starts in Warsaw – Lyft users from North America order rides in Poland in their own app, without installing Freenow. The rides are carried out by drivers working with Freenow.
- **Q1 2027:** start of the migration to the Lyft brand and Lyft's full platform in Europe. For some time both apps will run in parallel.
- **During 2027:** passengers will be gradually encouraged to move to the Lyft app; the Freenow name and logo are to disappear from cars.

Urban said users will not be forced to delete Freenow and install Lyft overnight. The pace is to depend on customers' reactions, as the Lyft brand is little known in Poland.

> The company has not yet said how driver and fleet-partner accounts will be migrated, or whether settlement rules and commissions will change. Status as of 5 November 2026.

The service model in Poland does not change: Freenow works with drivers who hold a passenger transport licence, often through partner companies (fleet partners) acting as carriers.

## Plans for 2027

Lyft has not disclosed an investment amount for Poland. According to Urban, the declared goals are:

- at least doubling the number of rides in Poland in 2027; Urban says the app-based ride market grows by 5–10% a year, so doubling would require – this is our conclusion – taking customers from competitors;
- entering 15–20 more smaller cities; Freenow currently operates in 25 cities, including about 17 smaller ones;
- strengthening its position in large cities;
- taking part in tenders for airports and railway stations, if they are announced;
- possible acquisitions of local companies – Urban does not rule them out but names no targets.

According to Urban, Freenow has been profitable in Poland since 2024 and works with over 10,000 active drivers; about one third of them are foreigners, most often from Ukraine.

In the longer term Lyft mentions autonomous vehicles. There are no such tests in Poland; Urban says the barrier is regulation, not technology. This is a statement of direction, not a timetable.

## What it means for fleets and drivers

For now the terms of cooperation with Freenow do not change, and the company has not announced new terms for drivers or fleet partners. The points below are our conclusions from the company's statements, not Lyft announcements.

- **Demand in smaller cities.** Entering 15–20 new cities means demand for licensed drivers and fleet partners where the platform has not operated so far. Urban points out that licensing times differ: in Warsaw about four months for a taxi licence and over a year for a driver ID, in Kraków and the Tricity two to three working days.
- **Stronger competition for drivers.** Aiming to double rides in a market growing 5–10% a year points to a fight with Uber and Bolt for passengers and drivers, including promotions.
- **North American tourists as new customers.** Roaming in Warsaw may add orders from the airport and hotels; the company has not said how many customers it expects.
- **App migration in 2027.** For fleets this may mean new tools (driver app, partner panel, settlement reports). No details yet.
- **Regulation.** Freenow opposes including taxis in the future Polish rules implementing Directive (EU) 2024/2831 and points to risks in the proposed presumption of employment and joint liability of platform and fleet partner. This is the company's position on draft rules, not law in force ([see: Poland will miss the 2 December deadline](/en/platform-work-directive-poland-will-miss-deadline)). It supports stricter verification of non-EU drivers, including criminal-record certificates from the country of origin.
