The Polish Association of App Partners (*Polski Związek Partnerów Aplikacyjnych*, PZPA), which brings together 21 companies that settle drivers' earnings and provide them with cars for work on ride-hailing apps, published a report on 17 November titled *Where are we heading? The ride-hailing industry yesterday, today and tomorrow* (in Polish). If its main proposal became law, every fleet partner would need a permit and a bank guarantee. The Polish act implementing the EU Platform Work Directive has not yet reached the Sejm (the lower house of parliament), and the transposition deadline is 2 December 2026.

## The report comes from a market player

Fleet partners are companies that sign contracts with drivers, settle their earnings from the platforms, handle the paperwork and often provide the car; a driver working through one does not need to run a business of their own. For background, see [our guide to the Polish taxi market](/en/polish-taxi-market-explained).

PZPA was set up in early 2024. According to the report, its members work with a total of over 40,000 people providing services through platforms (drivers and couriers). The report lists 21 member companies, including BonitoCars and SaltExpress, which were not on the list the association gave after its summer meeting ([our earlier report on PZPA, in Polish](https://pro.portaltaxi.pl/artykul/p58-pzpa-podwoil-liczbe-czlonkow-zwiazek-partnerow-aplikacyjnych-przed-ust)).

The report was prepared by an organisation that represents the interests of these companies. Its market value figures for 2023–2025 are the association's own estimates, and its proposals directly concern its members. In this text we keep the report's data separate from our own conclusions.

## The market according to PZPA

- Value of the app-based ride segment: from PLN 0.74bn in 2020 through PLN 2.5bn in 2022 to PLN 4.3bn in 2025. The 2020–2022 figures come from the TOR Economic Advisers Team (*Zespół Doradców Gospodarczych TOR*); 2023–2025 are PZPA estimates. The Jagiellonian Institute had earlier forecast about PLN 4.7bn for 2025.
- Share of apps: 80–83% of rides in the 20 largest cities (analysis from late 2025). Highest in Warsaw, the Tricity (Gdańsk, Gdynia, Sopot), Wrocław, Poznań, Kraków and Sosnowiec; lowest in Radom and Bydgoszcz. The report does not give this figure for the whole country.
- Users: in Q4 2025 Bolt had about 2.4m and Uber about 1.7m weekly active users (Sensor Tower data quoted in the report).
- PZPA members in 2025: over 29,000 drivers, over 6,000 cars, over 650 operational staff and offices in over 40 locations. They paid PLN 170m in taxes.

"The ride-hailing model, which until recently was a novelty on the market, now accounts for the majority of all taxi rides," the association's president writes in the report's introduction. The Polish daily *Rzeczpospolita* attributes this statement to Jarosław Antoniuk.

The platforms confirm the growth. Krzysztof Urban, managing director of Freenow by Lyft in Poland, told *Rzeczpospolita* that the number of rides is growing by 5–10% a year and that a taxi "is no longer a luxury good". Citing Dun & Bradstreet, the same paper reports that about two thirds of Polish taxi companies have financial problems. Tomasz Starzyk of D&B names high operating costs, taxes, fuel prices, car depreciation and staff costs as the causes.

## Prices: Poland fourth from the bottom among 12 countries

PZPA compared the prices of app-ordered rides in 12 European countries. A 10 km ride costs an average of PLN 39.13 in Poland, and a typical 5 km city ride PLN 19.55. The association also set prices against wages and calculated how many 100 km stretches a driver has to cover to meet the cost of living.

A 10 km ride in Poland costs 0.42% of the average monthly gross wage. The report gives a lower ratio only for Romania (0.22%) and Lithuania (0.35%). PZPA concludes that fares in Poland are too low and that drivers have to work much longer than in Western Europe. It adds that their costs are also rising because of clean transport zones.

Two caveats apply. PZPA notes that the prices were collected from publicly available sources and are not full app tariffs. And according to the report's own table of responsibilities, the fare for a ride is set by the platform, not by the partner or the driver. In practice, the call for higher fares is therefore addressed to Uber, Bolt and Freenow by Lyft.

## Proposal 1: a register of intermediaries and a permit for partner companies

PZPA proposes a Register of Digital App Intermediaries. Entry and a permit would depend on whether the company:

- is entered in CEIDG (the register of sole traders) or the National Court Register (KRS);
- has premises with access to original documents;
- provides a bank guarantee or surety of PLN 600,000;
- names at least one person authorised to represent it who lives in Poland, and a Polish address for service;
- has no tax or social security arrears, and its representatives have no convictions for certain offences, including offences against economic transactions and against the rights of people in paid work.

People who in the previous three years sat on the board of an intermediary struck off the register for unpaid liabilities would be temporarily barred from entry. Companies operating when the rules take effect would get at least 12 months to obtain an entry.

The association justifies the proposal with the practice of companies that "aggressively recruit collaborators, generate revenue and then disappear from the market", only to come back later under a new name. The guarantee is meant to cover drivers' claims and amounts owed to the state, and the premises requirement is meant to let inspectors see the documents in person.

> Our assessment: according to the report, more than half of drivers are served by a dozen or so of the largest companies, and the rest by hundreds or even thousands of smaller, often local businesses. For large partners a PLN 600,000 guarantee is probably manageable; for some small ones it may be a barrier. The proposal could curb dishonest practices, but it could also speed up market consolidation in favour of the largest companies, including PZPA members. The report does not present the views of smaller partners or estimate how many companies would meet the new conditions.

## Proposal 2: the same rules across the country

Today, municipalities set rules such as taxi markings and how rides are organised in local resolutions. PZPA wants these rules moved into national legislation, so that companies with the same business profile do not operate under different conditions depending on the city.

## Proposal 3: a central register of licences, taxis and taxi drivers

Data on licences, licence extracts (vehicle-specific documents issued under a licence), driver ID badges and vehicles are held in many separate registers, some only on paper. A company operating in several municipalities has to obtain a licence in each one separately and submit the same documents again. The report gives the example of Silesia: to drive legally across the conurbation, a driver has to carry dozens of licences or licence extracts. During inspections, officers phone a municipal official to check the driver and the documents.

![AI-generated illustration](pro/n-pzpa-raport-kontrola.jpg)

PZPA proposes a digital database updated in real time and linked to the Central Register of Vehicles and Drivers (CEPiK). Inspectors would then see at once, for example, withdrawn driving entitlements and expired roadworthiness tests. The idea is backed by Bartosz Pelczarski, head of the Transport Department at Poznań City Hall and president of the National Association of Heads of Transport Departments (*Ogólnopolskie Stowarzyszenie Szefów Wydziałów Komunikacji*). In his comment in the report, he calls the idea of central registers "a right solution that deserves support".

Pelczarski also points to risks: data security, the cost of rollout and maintenance, integration with municipal systems, and making local offices dependent on a single IT system. In his view the project needs fallback procedures and a clear division of responsibilities between central and local government.

## Background: two bills in progress

> Status as of 19 November 2026. Directive (EU) 2024/2831 on platform work: transposition deadline 2 December 2026. Draft act on platform work by the Ministry of Family, Labour and Social Policy: consultations completed, not yet sent to the Sejm. Draft UD431 by the Ministry of Infrastructure (amendments to the Road Transport Act): adoption by the Council of Ministers planned for Q1 2027.

The Ministry of Family, Labour and Social Policy presented its draft act implementing the directive in August ([our report on the draft](/en/platform-work-directive-poland-will-miss-deadline)). According to the business portal XYZ.pl, the ministry received over 700 comments, and an inter-ministerial reconciliation conference began on 4 November. On 9 November Minister Agnieszka Dziemianowicz-Bąk said (as reported by the PAP news agency) that the draft would go to the Sejm within a few months and that the rules should apply from next year. Legal adviser Robert Lisicki (C&C Chakowski & Ciszek) told XYZ.pl that Poland will not pass the act before the deadline.

The PZPA report sets out how responsibilities are split between market players. The platform is responsible for the technology, access to passengers and the fare; the partner for settlements, HR and administration. Both verify the driver before cooperation starts. This split matters for deciding whom the new rules will hold responsible for drivers' working conditions. *Rzeczpospolita* writes that PZPA member companies fear the platform work act will hit intermediaries.

Separately, work continues on the Ministry of Infrastructure's draft UD431, which among other things introduces a nationwide driver ID badge and requires intermediaries to check a taxi's roadworthiness test and third-party liability insurance ([details of the draft, in Polish](https://pro.portaltaxi.pl/artykul/p56-projekt-ud431-identyfikator-kierowcy-oc-taksowki-i-nowe-obowiazki-posr)). The draft and the proposed central register concern the same licensing system that fleet partners rely on.

## What next

- The deadline for transposing the Platform Work Directive is 2 December 2026. The Polish act is expected to reach the Sejm within a few months.
- PZPA's proposals are the position of an industry body, not draft legislation. It is not known whether a register of intermediaries will end up in the platform work act, in the amendment to the Road Transport Act, or in neither.
- Draft UD431 is due to go to the Council of Ministers in Q1 2027.
